# Assignment W2.3: Backup Your Computer

## Why Backing Up Your Computer is Important
Honestly, backing up your computer is the ultimate digital safety net that everyone knows they need, but usually ignores until it's too late. At any given moment, your laptop could take a spill, your hard drive could just randomly die, or you could get hit with malware that locks you out of everything. If you don't have a second copy of your files tucked away somewhere safe, a single bad day can mean losing everything permanently. Even just accidentally deleting an important folder can turn into a total disaster. Taking a few minutes to set up an automatic backup gives you massive peace of mind because if your computer explodes tomorrow, you can just download your life onto a new one and keep moving.

## Three Real-World Consequences of Missing Backups
* **Losing a business or client trust:** If a company loses its customer databases or financial records, they face massive downtime, angry clients, legal trouble, or straight-up bankruptcy.
* **Losing irreplaceable personal memories:** You instantly lose things you can never get back, like years of family photos, videos, and sentimental digital files.
* **Failing a class or losing weeks of work:** For students and professionals, a dead drive right before a major deadline means your project is gone, forcing you to scramble, ask for extensions, or fail.

## Backup Method Setup Steps: External Hard Drive (Windows file history)
1. **Plug it in:** Grab an external hard drive and connect it to your computer using a USB cable
2. **Find the settings:** Open your Windows Start Menu, type in "Backup settings," and open it up
3. **Pick your drive:** Click on "Add a drive" and select your external hard drive from the list that pops up
4. **Choose your folders:** Click "More options" to toggle on automatic backups and pick exactly which folders you want to save like Documents, Desktop, and Pictures.
5. **Run the first one:** Click "Back up now" to start the very first backup. Leave your computer plugged in until it finishes!

## Cloud Computing Backup Failure Case Study: UniSuper (2024)
* **What Happened:** In May 2024, an Australian retirement fund company called UniSuper suddenly went completely dark. Over 600,000 members were totally locked out of their accounts for a week, causing a massive panic.
* **The Root Cause:** It turned out to be a total nightmare scenario on Google Cloud's end. A Google engineer accidentally changed a setting during a routine account setup, which triggered an automated command that deleted UniSuper’s entire cloud account—including all of their active data and digital backups.
* **The Takeaway:** UniSuper thought they were safe because their data was backed up across two different geographic locations. But because both locations were on the exact same Google Cloud account, the deletion wiped out both at the exact same time. The only reason they survived is because they happened to have a completely separate, independent backup with a different provider. It proved that you should never keep all your eggs in one cloud basket.
